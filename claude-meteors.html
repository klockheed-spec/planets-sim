Entering debris stream
Each August, Earth passes through the debris trail of Comet 109P/Swift-Tuttle. Tiny particles — most no larger than a grain of sand — strike the atmosphere at 59 km/s, burning up as streaks of light.
As Earth rotates, your local sky turns to face the direction of orbital motion. By 2:00 AM, you're looking directly into the oncoming debris stream — like driving into snow — and the rate peaks near 60 meteors per hour.
The meteors appear to radiate from a point in Perseus — a perspective effect, like parallel railroad tracks converging in the distance.
